# VarsEL design

VarsEL helps people in Norway find the cheapest time to use electricity in their price area. Today opens with the current price and its time window. The price summary shows the cheapest period. The chart shows the shape of the day, while the table gives exact readings.

Use the shared sticky header, mobile dock, and footer on all routes. The header uses separate rounded surfaces for the brand, navigation, and controls, inspired by [emilb.no](https://emilb.no/). The surfaces become more solid as the page scrolls. The mobile dock uses the same styling, with visible route labels and an opaque fallback. Keep the reading surface solid and reserve only dock clearance below the footer on phones. The footer credits the source and author and shows connectivity. Keep the same price-area picker, unit labels, loading and retry treatments, and visible keyboard focus throughout. Heading and field icons use shared square wells. The Material Symbols font supplies glyphs while Quasar and the controls own their sizing and alignment. Display price intervals in Europe/Oslo. The Today, History, and Notifications views share full-day price data by area and date. A city selection supplies context for the same regional price.

Daylight uses a sage canvas, white reading surfaces, and an emerald default accent. Instrument Sans handles UI copy and headings. IBM Plex Mono marks prices, time windows, and chart axes. The top-right current price sits directly on the page with its label, unit, and time window. The price chart steps at the boundaries of published intervals. Solid panels, inset controls, sparse grid lines, and labelled chart markers keep the data legible. Dark mode uses a deep evergreen palette and both themes honor user-selected accent colors.

The visual direction draws on [Linear’s 2026 refresh](https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh), [Vercel’s typography system](https://vercel.com/geist/typography), and [Carbon’s chart guidance](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/data-visualization/axes-and-labels).

The location action sits below the Today area picker. It checks the device position once on request and compares it with a bundled NVE map. Match a price area only when the fix is sufficiently far from the boundary. Coordinates stay in the browser. Keep the manual pickers available during lookup and recovery.

`src/css/app.scss` in the frontend owns global surface, border, text, radius, chart, and scrollbar tokens. `MainLayout` owns navigation, install discovery, and page titles. `AppSelect` owns authored listboxes. History groups area, date, and hour filters in one panel. It uses Quasar’s localized calendar as a popover on desktop and a dialog on small phones. The chart and table show the last requested area and date. Varsler uses two parallel forms, with a shared weekday and quiet-hours section. Required-field errors sit under their controls. Saved alerts show their area, schedule, and enabled state. Quasar owns notification feedback and dialogs.

Om uses direct links to Today, History, and Alerts to explain the app. Today leads with the five price-area codes. Data-source links name what each source supplies, and the project panel credits the author and links to the repository.
